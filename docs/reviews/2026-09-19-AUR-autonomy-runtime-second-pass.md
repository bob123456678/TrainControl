### AUR-B1 - two roads that never meet are locked and fouled as one

**Where the fact is stated.** `src/org/traincontrol/automationui/TilePorts.java` 250 and 255: `DOUBLE_CURVE` and `FEEDBACK_DOUBLE_CURVE` are `route(N, W)` and `route(E, S)` - two curves in opposite corners of one square. SET-B2 line 70 reads them the same way: *"`DOUBLE_CURVE` two unconnected curves."* The file's own comment on `CROSSING` (268-271) says why a crossing's roads conflict - *"one physical crossing"* - and on `OVERPASS` (273-275) that grade separation is *"the single exception to 'shared tile means mutually exclusive'"*. A double curve is a second exception the comment does not know about: its two roads are as separate as an overpass's, only side by side rather than one above the other.

**Where the relation is built.** `GraphReducer.locationsOf` (1426-1442) gives every step one location id - the tile - and splits by route only for `OVERPASS`. `deriveLocks` (1370-1409) then locks every pair of edges that share an id, excusing only the two directions of one run (1403). `placesAlong` (1500-1517) writes the same id as the edge's place. So on a double curve with track on both roads, the edge over N-W and the edge over E-S are symmetric lock edges of each other, and both carry the square as a place.

**What the runtime in this package does with it** - three readers, all in scope:

1. *Route locks, needing no length.* `Edge.setOccupied` (612-620) cascades a claim to every lock edge; `Edge.isLockHeld` (522-525) answers on the count; `Layout.isPathClear` refuses a path over any held lock edge (2382-2391, `errorLockEdgeOccupied`). Path: `pickPath` 4509 or the right-click menu's `getPossiblePaths` 5484 -> `isPathClear` -> 2382. So while one train's locked route runs over the N-W curve, no train may be routed over the E-S curve of the same square - for the whole run with `atomicRoutes` on, and until the tail clears that edge with it off (`executePathInternal` 8023). On `1 - Main` that is 20,10, 21,10 and 11,11, whenever two routes want the two roads at once. Not a collision - the two trains would pass on separate rails - but a refusal the railway makes for no physical reason, and in a parallel timetable a wait that `executeTimetableInternal` (5834-5861) gives up on after `TIMETABLE_STUCK_MS` if the other road stays held.

2. *The shared-metal sweep, once measured.* `isPathClear` 2552-2609 refuses a path over an edge whose symmetric lock edge a standing train's tail covers, narrowed by `tailLiesOn` (7195-7203) to the places the tail claims - and `walkOneTail` claims the double-curve square (6969) as soon as the tail reaches it, so a train standing past a double curve refuses every path over the OTHER curve, which its body is not on. `passesTheTailsOfTrainsThatHaveNotMoved` (`HomeStaging` 1603-1632) and `passesTheTailsOfTrainsItHasMoved` (1549-1560) copy the rule, so Return Home refuses the same.

3. *The berth rule, once measured.* `whyABerthCannotHoldIt` (9037-9142): a berth whose approach runs over one curve of a double curve is refused, to every train whose tail reaches that square, on the lock loop at 9121-9137 - *"would foul road X"*, naming a road the train never touches. Both `isPathClear` (2672) and `firstClearRoute` (1348) ask it.

Readers 2 and 3 need lengths. They arrive together with the measurement: SET-B2's crossing step asks for every double curve with both roads on legs under *"Squares on this page that two roads cross"*, so the ordinary Mass Assign Lengths walk on `1 - Main` (MT-459) measures exactly these three squares - and, in the intermediate state RTX-C2 describes (crossing step done, a piece still at 0), a tail reaching a double curve refuses the other road to everybody. That is RTX-C2's shape with the wrong road refused as well as the right one; C2 below records the door.

**Why this is a B and not a ruling.** Nothing in `behaviour.md` or the reviews rules a double curve shared metal; the reducer's own comment (1334-1341) argues the overpass exception from *"the two tracks are at different heights"*, which is an argument about whether the rails meet, and a double curve's do not. SET-B2's ruling about double curves is about COUNTING a length once per road - right, and untouched by this - not about locking. The refusal costs throughput on the operating page today and will cost berths and paths the day the page is measured, on a shape Adam's railway has fifteen of across its pages (SVA-D4).

**Twins.** `FEEDBACK_DOUBLE_CURVE` is a Point, so its tile is an edge END and never a step (Edge 492-496; `placesAlong` adds the end tile separately at 1514, `deriveLocks` walks only `getPath()`), and no lock is derived from it - only the plain `DOUBLE_CURVE` reaches the relation. No other two-road tile in `TilePorts`' fixed list is non-touching (`CROSSING` touches; `OVERPASS` is already split).

**How to prove it.** In `core.testACoveredSwitchClosesTheOtherRoad`'s style, on a diagram fixture rather than a hand-built graph, because the relation is the reducer's: sensor A -> straight -> `DOUBLE_CURVE` by its N-W road -> sensor B, and sensor C -> straight -> the same square by its E-S road -> sensor D, no switch anywhere.

- Claim 1, no lengths: `configureAndLockPath(A->B, loc1)`, then assert `isPathClear(C->D, loc2)`. **Red today** with `errorLockEdgeOccupied`. Control on the same fixture with the square a `CROSSING`: refused, and must stay refused.
- Claim 2, measured: every tile at 1, a two-unit train standing at B having arrived along A->B (`executePath` on the fixture, or `setArrivedFrom`/`setArrivedAlong` by hand); assert `isPathClear(C->D, loc2)` and, with D marked not choosable, `whyABerthCannotHoldIt(C->D, loc2) == null`. Red today on both. Same crossing control.

**The fix is one method, outside this package:** `locationsOf` identifying `DOUBLE_CURVE` per route as it already does `OVERPASS`, so that a share of the SAME curve still locks and fouls and the other curve does not; `deriveLocks`, `placesAlong`, `tailLiesOn` and both walks follow without a change. The length side (SET-B2's `sharedSquaresALengthRuleReads`, which counts legs per tile and never reads a place id) is unaffected.

### AUR-C1 - a reservation inherits a tail nobody has

**Where.** `Layout.fromJSON` 11552-11567 writes `arrivedFrom` and `arrivedAlong` onto `landed` for every point the file names them on, whether or not a train was placed there - and the AMR-C3 drop three hundred lines up (11114-11134) is exactly the case where one was not: the placement is skipped and the log says so, *"everything else about the point ... is loaded as written."* That leaves a Point with `currentLoc == null`, a side and a road. RTX-D8 recorded this and called it harmless because *"no reader consults [them] without a locomotive and `setLocomotive` clears on the next occupant."* True of `setLocomotive` (570-574). Not true of the other door onto a Point: `Point.reserve` (593-600) calls `assign` (602-607), which sets `currentLoc` and nothing else.

**The path.** `configureAndLockPath` 3564 reserves every end of a locked route, the destination included -> the Point now has an occupant, the file's side and the file's road -> `walkStandingTrains` (6685-6695) walks *"every Point whose currentLoc is set"* with a positive train length, and hands `walkOneTail` (6713) the stale side and road -> the walk claims edges and places back along a road the reserving train has never driven. Readers of that claim: every `isPathClear` of every other train (`pickPath` 4509, the right-click menu 5484), and the covered-track picture (`AutonomySession` 5906 -> `edgesCoveredByStandingTrains`). It lasts until the train arrives (8201-8205 overwrite both) or the reservation is cleared (`setLocomotive(null)` in `unlockPath` or `handleMisconfiguredPath`), i.e. one run.

**What it costs, and what it needs.** Other trains are refused over the phantom's track with `errorTrackCoveredByStandingTrain` naming a train that is elsewhere - the refusing direction, one run long. It needs the first hop behind the square to be measured (6907 breaks before claiming otherwise) and the reserving train to have a length, so it is inert on the frozen railway. It needs a placement to have been dropped, which is a file outliving its fleet - the case Adam met on 2026-09-16 and ruled *"drop the train and keep the rest."*

**The second half, which is the record.** `Point.toJSON` writes `arrivedFrom` whenever it is set (1245-1247) and `arrivedAlong` likewise (1257-1261), with no occupant test - so the drop's own comment, *"the next save writes the file back without the phantom,"* is true of the placement and false of its tail: the side and road of a train that is no longer in the database are written back on every save, for ever, on a square that reads as empty. Each load rebuilds the state above.

**How to prove it.** In `core.testLayout`: build from a JSON whose point `P` names an unknown locomotive with `arrivedFrom` and `arrivedAlong`, every tile behind `P` measured at 1; assert the precondition `getPoint("P").getCurrentLocomotive() == null` and `getArrivedFrom() != null` (the drop left them). Lock a route ending at `P` for a two-unit train through `configureAndLockPath`; assert `placesCoveredByStandingTrains()` holds nothing for that train on the road behind `P`. **Red today.** Control: the same after a genuine `executePath` arrival at `P` must hold the places along the driven route. A second, cheaper claim: `toJSON()` of the loaded layout does not contain `arrivedFrom` for `P` - red today.

**Fix, either door:** `reserve` clearing both fields when the occupant changes, as `setLocomotive` does - that also settles the reserved dead-end case in D4 - or `fromJSON` writing a side and road only onto a square whose placement was honoured, with `toJSON` writing them only beside an occupant. The second is the smaller change and mends the record; the first mends every future way of leaving a side on an empty square.

### AUR-C2 - RTX-C2's state has a third door, and at a double curve it refuses the wrong road too

RTX-C2 traced the state *switch measured, piece behind it at 0* to two doors - `assignSwitchLength`'s separate step and MT-455's instruction to Skip - and showed that a berth or a parked train behind such a piece closes the switch's every road to every train with a length, because `whyABerthCannotHoldIt` (9083-9092) and `walkOneTail` (6907) both count one positive place as "measured" and then claim every unmeasured place for nothing. SET-B2 (`6d4aefee`, this morning) added a third step to the walk - one length for all of a page's crossings and double curves - and it runs AFTER the pieces, so a walk interrupted by Skip on a piece and then finished leaves the page in the same state with a crossing where RTX-C2 has a switch. MT-459 is the test whose steps produce it.

At a real crossing the refusal is at least of the right road: a train over the crossing does foul the other road. At a double curve it is B1's refusal - the other curve is a lock edge with the same place id - so the operator is told the berth *"would foul"* a road the train cannot reach. **How to prove it:** RTX-C2's own recipe with the switch tile replaced by a `CROSSING` at 1 and every other tile at 0, a one-unit train, assert `whyABerthCannotHoldIt` refuses; then the same with a `DOUBLE_CURVE` and the berth approached by one of its curves, assert the refusal names the other curve's road - both red today, and the second stays red after RTX-C2's remedy until B1 is fixed. Filed as an extension rather than a new finding: the ruling on both halves is RTX-C2's, and MT-455/MT-459 are where the sentence belongs.

### AUR-C3 - the visit history does not outlive a setup gesture

**Where.** `Layout.lastArrival` (449-453) is written at the end of every path (8163, keyed by `recencyKeyOf` 439-444 - the block, else the unique id) and read by `recencyOf` for `PathPreference.LEAST_RECENTLY_VISITED`. Its javadoc says *"kept in memory rather than saved: it describes this session's running."* A session, though, is longer than a `Layout`: `MarklinControlStation.parseAuto` (1018-1041) invalidates the old object and builds a new one with `Layout.fromJSON`, and it is reached from `AutonomyViewerPanel` 815 and `TrainControlUI` 23791 - which is every setup rebuild, including the one behaviour.md section 6a says the viewer's right-click autonomy menu makes *"after every gesture"*. `TrainControlUI` carries placements, sides, roads and the pending turns across that rebuild (6082-6184, 6469-6541, 7261-7309); nothing carries `lastArrival` (no reader of it outside `Layout` - `grep lastArrival\|noteArrival src/org/traincontrol/gui src/org/traincontrol/automationui` finds only the enum constant at `TrainControlUI` 11461).

**What the railway does.** With `LEAST_RECENTLY_VISITED` chosen, a run visits stations and builds the history; the operator stops, places a train or sets a facing from the diagram's right-click menu, and starts again - and every station is "never visited", so the rule is random until the history is rebuilt, and the far corner it promised to reach is not preferred over the platform the last run just left. The preference's own javadoc (below 439) describes that degraded state as the defect it was written to end. Refusing direction in the sense that matters - no wrong movement, a weaker choice - and only between runs, which is why it is a C.

**How to prove it.** `core.testLayout` or the preference's own class: `noteArrivalForTest("X")`, then rebuild the layout the way the window does (or call `Layout.fromJSON(layout.toJSON(), control)` and compare `recencyOf` on the two objects for a route to X); assert the new layout still ranks X as visited. Red today. The fix is the shape `putThePendingTurnsBack` already has: take the map out of the old layout in the rebuild bracket and put it into the new one, keyed by block or unique id as it is now.
