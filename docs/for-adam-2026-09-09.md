## The four you answered

### 1. "Unavailable While Occupied" and Return Home — your answer disagrees with what shipped

**You answered (a)**: Return Home is right to enforce the restriction, and the exception should be
written into the document.

**Nothing has been changed, because your own later ruling says the opposite and is already in the
code.** The timing is the whole of it, and it is worth having in front of you:

| when | what |
|---|---|
| 08 Sep 19:02 | this note was written, with the question open |
| 08 Sep 20:27 | `06d00a82` shipped, quoting you: *"enforce only in full autonomy. with the length checks, that is our primary anti collision mechanism, whereas the point exclusion is for modifying pathing prioritization."* |
| 09 Sep 07:27 | you answered **(a)** on the text above, which by then described a railway that no longer existed |

So the question you were reading had been settled an hour after it was written, the other way.
`HomeStaging` no longer asks the restriction at all, the runtime fence moved from `isAutoRunning` to
`isFullAutonomyRunning` so a staging run is not caught by it either, and `behaviour.md` §1 states it as
**"Occupancy restrictions are autonomy's alone"** with your sentence under it.

**If you still want (a)**, say so and it is small: the planner gets the check back through a
state-aware `canRest`, the runtime fence goes back to the wider flag so the two cannot disagree, and
§1 gains a second exception. It would also bring back OB-073 unless both halves move together, which
is what `HomeStaging`'s comment at that spot exists to say.

### 4. A compulsory turn is not something autonomy avoids — done

**Your answer: (b)** - the code is right and the table overstated it.

> Marking a square as a compulsory turn says what happens when a train ARRIVES, not who may send one
> there.

`behaviour.md` §3's row now reads *"yes, unless the square is ALSO marked one autonomy may not
choose"*, and the paragraph under it says the two markings are independent on purpose: a turning
square autonomy is welcome to use is one marking, a berth it must leave alone is the other, and every
square on your railway that turns trains carries both. That is why the ledger has looked as though one
implied the other.

`AutonomySession.stationsAutonomyWillNotChoose` claimed to be the runtime's own rule and is wider than
it - it counts a compulsory turn whether or not it is parking. The behaviour is left alone, because it
is a notice surface and it errs towards warning; the claim is corrected. Whether the editor should
narrow or the builder widen is **OB-195**, and it is yours.

## Fixed in this run

### MT-313 — Control+S named any tile, not just sensors

The right-click menu offers Rename only inside `if (isPoint)`. Control+S reached `promptNameFor`, which
asked whether the tile was null and nothing else — so the key put a naming dialog on plain track, where a
name is held by nothing and read by nobody. The predicate is `AutonomySession.canBeNamed` now and both
doors ask it. Tested against a copy of your real layout, because "which squares are Points" is exactly
what a hand-built fixture cannot say.

### OB-188 — the two shortcut buttons touched

The set-home strip was laid out with a horizontal gap of zero. `shortcutRow()` owns the layout now with a
named gap, and the test measures the pixels between the two buttons, with a zero-gap row built beside it
as the control.

### OB-187 — the menu bar settled in stages

The bar is greyed *before* it has finished being built. The autonomy menu is added later, from
`setViewListener`, which runs during the connect — so it arrived **enabled** into a bar where everything
else was grey, and stopped being grey at its own moment seconds early. One question now,
`menusAreHeldByTheNotice()`, which the menu asks when it mounts.

### MT-337 (2) — the home locomotive drawn twice

A station may legitimately carry two captions — a long platform labelled at both ends — and each caption
square asks about the station it names. A station captioned on its own square *and* on an offset one wrote
its home twice, side by side. The state arises without anyone asking for it: `setCaption` sweeps the old
caption away, `migrateStationLabels` writes through the raw store door and does not. `1 - Main:6,4` on your
railway is captioned on `6,5` and on `6,4`. A *self*-caption now gives way when another square already
names that station; two captions at opposite ends of a platform are both offset, so neither is touched.

**That square is TopMainR2, which is OB-193.** You said it was fixed and it is - by that change, made
the day before you asked. The check is in the suite now rather than in anybody's memory.

### MT-309 (2) — the shading that would not clear

**Not what it looked like.** The covered set *was* recomputed and the tiles *were* repainted — it was
recomputed to the wrong answer. Coverage is per **edge**, and an edge is one hop between two sensors, which
can be a dozen squares. A train of length 1 washed seventeen. The walk goes square by square now, each
paying its own length, stopping when the train is used up.

### MT-334 — the whole diagram redrew

The light redraw door added for OB-185 was wired to **one** caller, the One-Way tool. The four ordinary
ways to change a direction — including left-clicking track, the gesture you reported — still went through
the heavy one that rebuilds every label. Measured: 384 of 384 tiles destroyed and rebuilt for one arrow.

### MT-309 (1) — the orange line

The line replaces the wash, as you chose, and is drawn along the *road* the train is on rather than over
the square — which answers the double-curve complaint directly. That answer already existed in the data:
the reducer records which route each step runs through.

### MT-262 — a berth too short refuses the train

The room rule existed but was fenced: asked only where the destination was a terminus or a reversing
square, so a plain through platform was never judged on the track leading into it. Measured on your
railway: `75 407 DB` at BottomMainPost was offered `BottomMainA (eastbound)` with one measured unit behind
it. Both length rules are one predicate now, `whyTooLongForTheBerth`, which returns the sentence — the
berth, the room found, the train's length — so the refusal is readable rather than "check the log", and it
reaches the why-not-moving view.

### MT-247 — only the switch under the train is skipped

`execRoute` read the ruling as being about the whole accessory *group*: it asked once, before the loop, for
the **first** held command, then skipped every accessory. One turnout under a train dropped every other
turnout in the route. Each accessory is asked about immediately before it goes out now, and only the held
one is skipped. The emergency-stop carve-out from SVN-A4 is untouched.

### OB-194 — the bulk clear says what it will do

Your ruling: *"Warning if using the bulk tool."* The confirmation named nothing; it now names every
locomotive it is about to lift, says how many squares it will empty, and says that **Cancel will not
put them back**. It cannot, and the reason is your own ruling of the day before - since OB-183 a
placement is read from the railway rather than from the setup, so Cancel restores the file and not the
trains. One builder feeds both the dialog and the menu item's tooltip, so the warning cannot arrive
only after the click.

### FR-068 — the bracket that is not the first term can be built

Your question: *"it should be representable already in the UI, right?"* Yes, and nothing needed
changing. Seven condition lines typed flat and indented twice build `3 or (4 and (1 or 2))` - your
shape with the AND written the other way round. The only thing that cannot be **typed** is the exact
outline the loader writes when the group is the AND's left child, because a line may be at most one
level deeper than the line above it; a route carrying that opens and reads correctly, which is what
MT-320 settled.
